    02 / THE BASICS

    How your roof becomes a water source

    Rainwater harvesting captures rain where it falls, filters out debris, and stores the clean runoff for useful non-potable needs. It also slows stormwater and helps recharge the ground.

    1. COLLECT Roof surfaces guide rain into gutters and downpipes.
    2. FILTER A leaf screen and first-flush diverter remove early dust and debris.
    3. STORE OR RECHARGE Use a covered tank, or direct overflow into a recharge pit.
    04 / CALCULATION

    How your estimate is calculated

    WATER = ROOF AREA × RAINFALL × RUNOFF COEFFICIENT × COLLECTION EFFICIENCY

    Annual harvestable water (L) = roof area (m²) × annual rainfall (mm/year) × roof runoff coefficient × collection efficiency. For example, 100 m² × 650 mm × 0.70 × 0.85 = 38,675 L/year. Actual yield depends on rainfall timing, first-flush losses, overflow, and system condition; the example is illustrative, not locality data.

    03 / PRACTICAL GUIDE

    Make your system safer and more useful

    01

    Keep it covered

    Use a sealed, shaded tank with a mosquito-proof vent. Label stored water clearly and keep it separate from drinking-water lines.

    02

    Clean before monsoon

    Clear leaves from the roof and gutters, inspect joints, and test the first-flush outlet before heavy rain begins.

    03

    Use every litre wisely

    Harvested water is useful for gardening, cleaning, flushing, and recharge when local rules and treatment requirements are followed.

    04 / BENEFITS

    Why rainwater harvesting matters

    💧

    Water Security

    Reduce dependence on municipal supply and store rainwater for irrigation, flushing, and emergency use.

    🌿

    Groundwater Recharge

    Decrease runoff and improve recharge with filtration systems that restore local aquifers.

    ⚡

    Lower Costs

    Plan tank size and system design more accurately so you invest in the right capacity for your roof.
